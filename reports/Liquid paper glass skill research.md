# Pin the layers, free the axes, test every world

A reusable "liquid paper glass" skill should teach a **layer contract and a token schema, not a look**. The contract has two layers: an opaque paper/ink content layer and a floating glass chrome layer. Apple's own rules already draw that line, so the hybrid is consistent with Apple's guidance ([WWDC25 219](https://nonstrict.eu/wwdcindex/wwdc2025/219/)). Everything a world changes then sits in independent token groups: palette, glass variant, paper cut, type pairing, motion personality and light. Only the light is global. The skill must also ship validators, because the axes interact: a glass variant that passes with one palette fails with another. Four limits shape the advice below. First, **Apple publishes almost no numbers** for blur, opacity, radii or timing, so every pixel value here is a tunable starting point, labeled as such. Second, **no sourced case study** of an illustrated or hand-drawn iOS 26 app exists in the notes, so the paper-plus-glass hybrid and the ink key line on glass are inference. Third, the Silksong example in this repo is a working implementation, not independent validation. Fourth, native refraction exists only on iOS 26 and, with caveats, in Impeller and Chromium, so every platform recipe needs a flat fallback tier. Tags used throughout: **[SRC]** sourced fact, **[REPO]** value shipped in this repository, **[INF]** inference or tuning value with no source.

## Apple's split gives the skill a defensible spine

Apple describes Liquid Glass as a "distinct functional layer" for controls and navigation floating above content. Large content such as tables stays in the content layer ([WWDC25 219](https://nonstrict.eu/wwdcindex/wwdc2025/219/)). The HIG wording, read through a mirror and not apple.com, adds that glass does not belong in the content layer. The one exception is transient controls such as slider and toggle thumbs, which may take glass only while active ([HIG Materials](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/materials)). Paper as the content layer therefore maps one-to-one onto Apple's hierarchy: cards, rows, pages, illustration and body type are paper; tab bar, circle buttons, sheets, alerts and switches are glass **[INF]**. Several further rules are sourced and should be encoded as hard constraints. Never nest glass on glass, and never mix Regular and Clear ([WWDC25 219](https://nonstrict.eu/wwdcindex/wwdc2025/219/)). Tint only the one primary action, and color its background, not its symbol or text ([HIG Color](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/color)). A capsule's radius is half its height, and a nested radius is the parent radius minus padding ([WWDC25 356](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/356/)). Hit targets are at least 44x44 pt ([HIG Buttons](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/buttons)). Drop custom bar backgrounds, because they fight the glass and the scroll edge effect ([Adopting Liquid Glass](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/technologyoverviews/adopting-liquid-glass)). Light weights (Ultralight, Thin, Light) are discouraged on materials ([HIG Typography](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/typography)).

Regular glass should be the default in a themed skill. Clear needs three conditions: media-rich content behind, a tolerable dimming layer, and bold content above ([WWDC25 219](https://nonstrict.eu/wwdcindex/wwdc2025/219/)). A flat paper backdrop rarely meets them **[INF]**. The one concrete number Apple states is a **35% dark dimming layer** for Clear glass over bright content, and it is unnecessary over dark content ([HIG Materials](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/materials)).

The skill's own additions are inference, and it should say so. A glass part stacks, bottom to top: backdrop blur, paper-tone scrim, tint (primary only), inner shadow on the side opposite the light, sheen gradient, specular rim on the lit edge, ink key line, then glyph or label **[INF]**. The scrim is the world-colored version of Apple's dimming layer. The key line, about 1 to 1.5 px in the same ink token as the paper outlines, makes chrome and content read as one world. It also supplies the 3:1 boundary that critics say Apple's faint edge lacks ([UX Collective](https://uxdesign.cc/did-apple-abandoned-its-own-design-heuristics-accessibility-principles-2d616ed7ace5), opinion; WCAG 1.4.11 sets the 3:1 rule, [W3C](https://w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/non-text-contrast)). Paper itself is recognized by five traits **[INF, from practitioner pages]**: opaque fills, one consistent ink line weight, a hard zero-blur offset shadow, slight irregularity such as jittered corners or fill/line misregistration, and a serif or editorial display face. Practitioner sources describe the same recipe: borders of about 2 to 5 px and zero-blur offset shadows ([Superdesign](https://www.superdesign.dev/styles/brutalism/components)), and warm off-white stock, few spot colors, grain and imperfect registration for riso ([riso skill listing](https://skillselion.com/skills/bergside/awesome-design-skills/riso)). No primary or academic source defines "paper UI".

## Seven axes stay independent only if the interfaces are narrow

The notes found no sourced analysis of which combinations users judge incoherent, so the axis design below is inference. The best structural precedent is Material You, where seed, style, contrast level and brightness are independent inputs to one scheme ([Material Color Utilities](https://github.com/material-foundation/material-color-utilities)). The second is Apple's user-level Clear/Tinted choice layered over any app palette ([TidBITS](https://tidbits.com/?p=72096)). The skill should define one small immutable group per axis **[INF]**:

| Axis | Token group | Concrete starting values | Provenance |
|---|---|---|---|
| World palette | 22 paper roles plus 4 glass roles, explicit hex per brightness | See the Silksong section | [REPO] |
| Glass variant | Regular / Tinted / Flat; blur sigma, fill alpha, rim alpha, saturation, key-line width | Regular: blur 12-20, saturate 1.6-1.8, fill 0.05-0.15 dark or 0.55-0.65 light, rim 0.2-0.35 dark; Tinted: fill 0.5-0.7; Flat: scrim 0.92-1.0, no blur | [INF], tuned from community recipes |
| Paper cut | Radius tiers, stroke width, shadow offset, jitter | Tiers 8 / 6 / 4 with 1 px edge; neobrutalist alternative 2-5 px stroke with hard offset | [REPO]; [SRC practitioner] |
| Type pairing | Display + text family, scale, weights | One serif display, one grotesk text, max two families; avoid light weights on glass | [REPO]; weights [SRC] |
| Motion personality | Paper stepper, glass spring, durations | Paper on twos (about 140 ms steps); glass spring response about 0.4, damping about 0.8, press scale 1.03-1.08 | steps [REPO]; spring [SRC single author]; scale [INF] |
| Light | One vector, drives rim, sheen, inner shadow, paper shadow | Upper-left default; tilt in [-1, 1] on each axis | [REPO]; -60 degree default [SRC kube.io] |

The spring values come from one community SwiftUI example, and the 1.03-1.08 press scale is a guess; Apple publishes no durations, curves or spring constants ([Medium reference](https://medium.com/@veersr9/ios-26-liquid-glass-in-swiftui-a-practical-reference-demo-app-liquidglassreference-69b8d46dc055)). Tinted fill 0.5 to 0.7 is a guess too, because Apple does not publish the Tinted or Reduce Transparency opacities ([Fast Company SA](https://fastcompany.co.za/tech/2025-11-10-how-to-adjust-apples-liquid-glass-in-ios-26/)).

Mix-and-match is safe only with validators. Seven coherence rules follow from the structure **[INF]**. Glass over a palette must keep label contrast against the worst-case backdrop. Icon stroke weight should sit within one step of the border stroke. Heavy display faces pair with larger radius tiers. Heavy glass gets slower, softer curves, and every personality collapses to roughly zero duration under reduced motion. One light vector feeds every shadow, rim and sheen. The dark variant keeps the hue family and light direction rather than inverting. Presets are curated recipes, while a "mix" mode runs the validators and warns instead of blocking.

## From a world to hexes without importing the world

Extraction should propose, a human should decide, and literals should win. Material Color Utilities offers a ready pipeline: quantize to N colors (Celebi, Wu then WSMeans), score for UI suitability, then build HCT tonal palettes ([GitHub](https://github.com/material-foundation/material-color-utilities)). OKLCH is the better audit space, with consistent lightness steps across hues, but it cannot replace contrast checks; the article itself points to APCA for detecting contrast ([Evil Martians](https://evilmartians.com/chronicles/oklch-in-css-why-quit-rgb-hsl)). The pipeline the skill should teach is **[INF]**: gather 5 to 15 reference images, quantize and k-means in OKLab for dominant hues and surface/ink extremes, weight by chroma so small accents survive, let a human assign roles, store the chosen hexes as literals, and log the source images in a provenance manifest. Seed-derived schemes re-derive every role, so pinned brand hexes drift. This repo avoids that by mapping `ColorScheme` by hand rather than using `fromSeed` ([REPO: THEME_PLAN.md D2]). Tokens use three tiers, primitives then semantic roles then optional component tokens, and the Flutter carrier is a `ThemeExtension` with `copyWith` and `lerp`, which cross-fades a theme switch ([Flutter API](https://api.flutter.dev/flutter/material/ThemeExtension-class.html)). Sourcing is thin for the three-tier model (third-party blogs only). Lerping in sRGB can go muddy at mid-tones, so a skill may lerp in OKLab, which is inference.

The world's source is often thinly documented, which the skill must expect. For Silksong, **no source gives color values for any area**. Only about a dozen of roughly 30 biomes have any published visual description, and the rest are inferred from names ([repo report: Silksong Pharloom biomes.md](../reports/Silksong%20Pharloom%20biomes.md)). The skill should therefore require sampling official screenshots and then running the contrast gates, never trusting recalled colors.

IP hygiene belongs in the skill as a pre-flight checklist; it is practical guidance, not legal advice. Fan art is an unlicensed derivative work, tolerated when wholesome but often taken down in commercial contexts ([Plagiarism Today](https://www.plagiarismtoday.com/2025/07/29/the-pokemon-plagiarism-controversy/), [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2022/01/24/fan-made-game-escapes-nintendo)). The notes could not retrieve the US Copyright Office sources, so the following is **[INF]**: take palette and mood, not assets; use generic evocative theme names; ship no logos, sprites, screenshots, lifted icons, sounds or original fonts; use OFL or Apache fonts that match the mood; keep a provenance manifest; and ship only original presets in any user-built "own world" feature. This repo already follows the pattern by crediting feel, "never its art, characters or fonts" ([REPO: DESIGN.md](../DESIGN.md)).

## Platform recipes share one three-tier ladder

Every platform should implement the same ladder: premium (real refraction), standard (blur + saturation + rim), flat (opaque scrim and key line). The ladder is inference, but each rung is backed by a sourced capability.

| Platform | Premium | Standard | Flat trigger | Source quality |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| SwiftUI / UIKit | `glassEffect(.regular.tint(_).interactive(), in: shape)` inside one `GlassEffectContainer`; `glassEffectID` + `@Namespace` to morph; `.buttonStyle(.glass)` / `.glassProminent` | `if #available(iOS 26, *)` else `.background(.ultraThinMaterial, in: shape)` plus manual tint and press scale | System honors Reduce Transparency, Increase Contrast, Reduce Motion for native glass | Container, ID, tint, styles [SRC] ([WWDC25 323](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/323/)); fallback [INF]; `UIDesignRequiresCompatibility`, `glassEffectUnion`, UIKit class names unverified |
| Flutter | `liquid_glass_renderer` (Impeller only, 16 shapes per blend group, pre-release) or `liquid_glass_widgets` premium tier | `BackdropFilter` with `ImageFilter.compose` (saturation matrix + blur), tint, 1 px gradient stroke, `ClipRRect`, grouped with `BackdropGroup` / `BackdropFilter.grouped` | `MediaQuery.highContrast`, `disableAnimations`; Reduce Transparency needs a platform channel | Packages and `isShaderFilterSupported` [SRC] ([pub.dev](https://pub.dev/packages/liquid_glass_renderer), [pub.dev](https://pub.dev/packages/liquid_glass_widgets), [BackdropGroup](https://api.flutter.dev/flutter/widgets/BackdropGroup-class.html)); sigma and matrix values [INF] |
| Web | SVG `feDisplacementMap` via `backdrop-filter: url(#lens)`, Chromium only | `backdrop-filter: blur(16px) saturate(1.7)`, translucent fill, inset rim, Baseline 2024 | `@media (prefers-reduced-transparency: reduce), (prefers-contrast: more)` and `@supports not (backdrop-filter: blur(1px))` | [MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/Reference/Properties/backdrop-filter), [kube.io](https://kube.io/blog/liquid-glass-css-svg/), [caniuse](https://caniuse.com/mdn-css_at-rules_media_prefers-reduced-transparency) |
| Compose | Haze `hazeGlass` (v2, beta) | `Modifier.hazeSource` / `hazeBlur`, always with tint and border | API below 31 unverified | [Haze](https://github.com/chrisbanes/haze); minimum-API behavior unverified |
| React Native | `expo-glass-effect` `GlassView` or `@callstack/liquid-glass`, iOS 26 only | `expo-blur` (not fetched) or translucent View | Check `isLiquidGlassAvailable()` and `AccessibilityInfo.isReduceTransparencyEnabled()` | [Expo](https://docs.expo.dev/versions/latest/sdk/glass-effect.md), [Callstack](https://www.callstack.com/blog/how-to-use-liquid-glass-in-react-native) |

Three platform traps deserve explicit mention in the skill. On the web, a parent with `filter`, `opacity` below 1, `mask`, `clip-path` or `mix-blend-mode` bounds what a child's backdrop-filter sees, so a parent at `opacity: 0.9` can make the glass look inert ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/Reference/Properties/backdrop-filter)). Also, `prefers-reduced-transparency` ships in Chromium 118+ only, with Firefox disabled by default and no Safari support ([caniuse](https://caniuse.com/mdn-css_at-rules_media_prefers-reduced-transparency)), so `prefers-contrast` and a manual toggle must back it up. In Flutter, creating an `ImageFilter.shader` throws when Impeller is off, so gate on `ImageFilter.isShaderFilterSupported` ([Flutter API](https://api.flutter.dev/flutter/dart-ui/ImageFilter/isShaderFilterSupported.html)), and `liquid_glass_widgets` documents that Flutter does not expose Reduce Transparency directly ([pub.dev](https://pub.dev/packages/liquid_glass_widgets)). The web refraction recipe itself is community-sourced: Snell's law with n=1.5 over a convex squircle bezel, 127 ray samples encoded into an `feImage` displacement map, with `scale` matched to maximum displacement and the map sized to the element ([kube.io](https://kube.io/blog/liquid-glass-css-svg/)). The cheap `feTurbulence` variant gives random wobble rather than a lens. Without refraction and a responsive rim, the result is ordinary glassmorphism ([LogRocket](https://blog.logrocket.com/how-create-liquid-glass-effects-css-and-svg/)).

Performance has no published benchmark on any platform, so the budget is heuristic **[INF]**. Use 2 to 4 glass surfaces per screen, never glass inside list rows, one shared backdrop pass, and a one-time snapshot blur for sheets over static content. Skip blur whenever the backdrop is flat, since it would be invisible anyway. The sourced pieces are the shared-sampling rule (group in a container), the 16-shape cap, and Callstack's advice to apply glass to floating controls rather than full-screen backgrounds ([Callstack](https://www.callstack.com/blog/how-to-use-liquid-glass-in-react-native)). Grain should be one static tiled texture under a repaint boundary, never animated, since a constantly changing layer defeats Flutter's raster cache (low-authority source: [omr.it.com](https://omr.it.com/blog/flutter-engine-raster-cache-tuning/)).

## Accessibility fallbacks are first-class states with their own tested values

Apple states three automatic fallbacks. Reduce Transparency makes glass "frostier" and hides more background. Increase Contrast makes elements mostly black or white with a contrasting border. Reduce Motion lessens effects and disables elastic properties ([WWDC25 219](https://nonstrict.eu/wwdcindex/wwdc2025/219/)). The critique justifies building these in rather than bolting them on. NN/g documents illegible text over busy backgrounds, crowded tab bars and unprompted motion ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/liquid-glass/)). Apple's own correction was frostier betas and then the iOS 26.1 Clear/Tinted setting ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2025/10/20/ios-26-1-beta-4-adds-new-setting-to-tone-down-liquid-glass-transparency/)). Contrast figures for the shipped material are anecdotal. One audit's "as low as 1.5:1" has no stated method ([Infinum](https://infinum.com/blog/apples-ios-26-liquid-glass-sleek-shiny-and-questionably-accessible/)). The sources conflict on how 26.1 and 26.2 interact with Reduce Transparency, so the skill should not assume system behavior.

The skill should therefore define a state ladder for every glass part, with values being **[INF]** starting points:

| State | Trigger | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Regular | Default | Blur 12-20, saturate 1.6-1.8, scrim 0.55-0.8 in paper tone, key line 1-1.5 px |
| Tinted | User toggle (mirrors 26.1) | Fill 0.5-0.7, stronger rim, same key line |
| Reduce Transparency | OS flag or platform channel | Scrim about 1.0, no blur, key line kept |
| Increase Contrast | `highContrast` / `prefers-contrast: more` | Flat fill, key line 2 px, ink-on-paper labels, no sheen |
| Reduce Motion | `disableAnimations` / `prefers-reduced-motion` | Light frozen at upper-left, no sensor tilt or idle drift, springs critically damped, paper stepping becomes instant or a single crossfade |

Further rules apply across states. The default theme for a paper backdrop should be the Tinted equivalent. Chrome should never carry ambient looping motion, only input-driven motion, per the NN/g critique. Text scrolling under chrome needs a scroll-edge fade or an opaque paper strip to avoid text-on-text (Apple's soft scroll edge effect is the model: [HIG Scroll views](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/scroll-views)). Do not put text on glass directly over illustration without a scrim. Dark worlds need special care, because near-black key lines vanish on dark paper and must invert to a lighter ink or gold, then be re-checked at 3:1 **[INF]**.

## Test gates turn taste into pass or fail

Flutter's test guidance gives the base: widget tests default to the Ahem font, gallery goldens load real fonts, run on one platform because text rendering differs, and make shadows opaque to reduce flakiness ([Flutter gallery](https://dart.googlesource.com/external/github.com/flutter/gallery/+/8f5d333cc8d61affe07204b262f3f2d9b5b4a607/test_goldens)). WCAG supplies the thresholds: 4.5:1 for normal text and 3:1 for large text under 1.4.3, computed unrounded so 4.499 fails ([W3C 1.4.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html)), and 3:1 for component boundaries under 1.4.11 ([W3C](https://w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/non-text-contrast)). The gates below combine those with inference **[INF]**:

| Gate | Check | Failing means |
|---|---|---|
| Role contrast | Per world x brightness: text, muted, onAccent on their fills at least 4.5; accent, outline, focus, danger vs surface at least 3 | A palette ships illegible |
| Glass worst case | Composite scrim over lightest and darkest plausible backdrop (tokens, no pixels), then label at least 4.5 and key line / rim vs surround at least 3, in Regular, Tinted, Reduce Transparency and High Contrast | The glass variant is incompatible with the world |
| Pinned hexes | Designer-chosen roles equal declared literals; derived roles flagged as derived | Seed drift |
| No raw colors | Scan source for color literals outside the token file | Axis leakage |
| Light singleton | Every glass part and paper shadow read one light; one test asserts shadow direction matches rim side | Mixed light directions |
| Reduced motion | With animations disabled, tilt and drift return a constant and paper steppers finish in zero frames | Motion fallback broken |
| Layout | Targets at least 44 pt; no overflow at 200% text; goldens for each part over backdrop matrix (lightest paper, darkest ink, busy illustration) at scale 1.0 and 2.0, zero tolerance for hard-shadow paper, small tolerance for blur | Layout regression |
| Color-blind | State pairs differ by at least 3:1 luminance plus a non-color cue (icon, label, shape) | Hue-only state coding |

APCA should run as warn-only, since it is a WCAG 3 candidate still in development ([APCA intro](https://git.apcacontrast.com/documentation/APCAeasyIntro)). The luminance-separation threshold is a heuristic with no authoritative source. Items that cannot be automated must be listed as explicit manual checks: real gyro feel, blur frame time on an older device, grain on OLED, 200% text, each OS accessibility toggle, VoiceOver order, sunlight legibility, and the Clear/Tinted setting.

## Silksong shows the contract working, and where it is unproven

This repository is the only worked example, and its evidence is implementation, not outside validation. The five biomes (Pharloom, Deep Docks, Choral Chambers, Moss Grotto, Greymoor) are explicit `const` palettes with both brightnesses chosen per biome, not computed by inversion, with light worlds for Choral and Moss ([REPO: THEME_PLAN.md D1-D4](../THEME_PLAN.md)). Each palette holds 22 paper roles plus four glass roles: `glassFill`, `glassFillHi`, `glassLight`, `glassKey`. In dark biomes the repo uses a white fill at alpha 0x14 (about 8%), a hi fill at 0x29 (about 16%), and a warm-white light at 0xD9, all in `app/lib/core/palette.dart`. In the light biomes fill is 0x99 (about 60%) and hi is 0xE6 (about 90%) with a pale paper-tinted white. The glass key line is each world's near-black ink (for example `#050307` in Pharloom, `#2A1E0F` in Choral) **[REPO]**. Shape is one needle-cut language: a 1 px edge with radii 8 / 6 / 4, state changing edge color and never shape ([REPO: DESIGN.md](../DESIGN.md)). Type is Cormorant Garamond for display and Hanken Grotesk for UI **[REPO]**. Motion pairs held paper redraws, three jittered ink frames stepped every 140 ms, with continuous glass. The glass surface uses blur sigma 16 default, saturation 1.7, a `.35` black shadow with blur 14, and a flat shell fill under high contrast **[REPO: glass_surface.dart]**. The single light is a `GlassTilt` notifier in [-1, 1] on each axis, with idle drift `x = sin(0.7t) * 0.8`, `y = cos(0.5t) * 0.5` and frame-rate-independent easing at 12% per 60 Hz frame **[REPO: glass_tilt.dart]**. These numbers sit inside the community ranges above, which is encouraging but not proof, since both are tuned by eye.

Three cautions apply to the example. First, the world input is weak: no source gives Silksong color values, so the palettes are design-canvas choices informed by names and screenshots. Second, a custom Flutter glass cannot use the system material and emulates it, so it is glassmorphism-plus-rim rather than true lensing unless a shader tier is added. Third, light-biome glass sits at 60% fill, which suits the "Tinted by default for paper" recommendation but has not been contrast-gated against all backdrops in this research.

## Conclusion

The research changes the skill's job from "make glass look like Apple's" to "make a contract that survives any world". Apple's physics are qualitative and its numbers are absent, so the portable asset is the rule set (layer split, one tint, no nesting, concentric radii, three fallbacks) plus a tuned token schema with validators. The hybrid's most distinctive features, the ink key line, the paper-tone scrim and the stepped-paper versus spring-glass motion contrast, are inference and should be presented to users as defaults to test, not as established practice.

Four gaps decide what to verify next. Sample real iOS 26 screenshots or Apple's Figma kit to replace the tuning values. Confirm the Clear/Tinted and Reduce Transparency interaction on current iOS. Measure blur cost on a low-end Android device, where no benchmark exists. Check the unverified Swift and Compose API details (`glassEffectUnion`, `UIDesignRequiresCompatibility`, Haze minimum API) in Xcode headers and library docs. Until then, the safest shipped default for any world is Tinted glass over paper, with the key line always on and the flat tier one switch away.
